# Shot and Prompt Examples

## Example 1: Preserve a breakup beat and add meaningful resistance

Input: “Boyfriend says it is over. Girlfriend cries. Cut to gift reveal.”

Instead of: “She is devastated, sobbing dramatically, shaking, tears streaming.”

Director approach:

- Goal: persuade him to stay without appearing desperate.
- Resistance: she fears humiliation and assumes he is bluffing.
- Trigger: he does not withdraw the breakup statement.
- Tactic: hold a false smile, then try to speak and stop.
- Primary visible action: the attempted smile settles as she recognizes he is serious.
- Support: interrupted inhale; hand hovering near an unopened gift, only if it is present in the shot.
- Exit state: resolve visibly cracks; the next cut can reveal the object without inventing a different narrative.

Production-ready example:

> Cinematic intimate medium close-up, restrained realistic performance. She listens to his final sentence, trying to keep a reassuring smile because she wants him to stay. He remains silent. The smile slowly falls; she draws breath to speak but chooses not to. Maintain her previous eye-line, hairstyle, outfit, and the same night street lighting. Hold on her reaction without rapid head movement. Avoid forced tears or exaggerated facial contortions.

## Example 2: Comedy without forced melodrama

Input: “A child claims the living-room floor is lava; parent enters.”

The child's objective is to protect the game world from adult interruption. The parent wants safe, practical order. Humor arises from the collision of sincere high stakes with mundane reality. Expressions need not escalate to crying. The child hears the doorknob, immediately freezes, then calmly tries to recruit the parent as an ally. Keep prop and room-layout continuity.

## Example 3: Cinematic advertisement

Input: “Resident opens an apartment window and imagines what living here is like.”

Avoid instructing “look happy.” Assign an objective: test whether this place can feel like home. A small held breath, a hesitant move to the window, and shoulders relaxing only after a meaningful sight or sound can make the moment credible. Do not invent property claims or imply documentary authenticity for staged AI footage.

## Example 4: Finished-film critique

A good critique distinguishes:

- verified visual observation;
- narrative reason the reaction does or does not work;
- one concrete corrective shot instruction;
- what to preserve from the current edit.

Never claim frame-by-frame comparison if only promotional descriptions or inaccessible links were provided.

## Example 5: Short directives by deliverable

- User: “Give me 8 prompts, each 5 seconds.”
  - Response: 8 executable prompts; compact entry and exit state annotations only where necessary; no seven-level exposition.
- User: “Just check the dialogue.”
  - Response: Dialogue with subtext-aware timing; do not silently rewrite the plot.
- User: “Compare these two reels.”
  - Response: Grounded comparison in acting, continuity, and storytelling; limitations stated.
- User: “What's the rent?”
  - Response: This skill does not activate.

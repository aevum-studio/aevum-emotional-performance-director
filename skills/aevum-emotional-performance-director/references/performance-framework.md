# AEVUM Emotional Performance Framework — Director's Reference

## Foundations (practice, not imitation)

The skill synthesizes useful principles from professional film acting and actor-training traditions, including attentive listening and camera-scale restraint; given circumstances and lived backstory; objectives and subtext; contradictory external behavior; preparation that enables spontaneity; and strategically held or released emotion. These are practical design principles, **not instructions to imitate a named actor's identity or signature performance**.

## Seven-level acting analysis

### 1. Character Psychology

- Given circumstances: what happened, who knows what, physical and social environment.
- Objective or verb: persuade, protect, conceal, test, repel, reconnect; prefer playable actions to feelings.
- Stakes: what's personally at risk right now?
- Subtext: spoken wording versus hidden intention.
- Resistance: pride, fear, relationship history, physical obstacle, competing objective.
- Contradiction: “Leave me alone” may coexist with trying to keep someone close.

### 2. Emotional Causality

Do not start by asking “what expression?” First identify stimulus and interpretation. Example:

- A: Partner announces they're leaving.
- B: Character initially interprets this as provocation and counters with a smile.
- C: Partner silently turns to walk away.
- D: Character realizes the threat is real; the attempt at sarcasm collapses.
- E: Character reaches, stops halfway, and changes strategy to denial.

The emotional direction can be escalation, suppression, reversal, numbness, humor under pressure, or relief.

### 3. Physical Performance

- Render acting decisions, not pseudo-scientific emotion-detection claims.
- Underplay close-ups; the camera amplifies small choices.
- Listening is active behavior: track whose words are being processed, whether the eyes stay or avert, and the response delay.
- Use timing and muscular change sparingly. Avoid simultaneity overload such as blink + frown + cry + shake + gasp + reach every second.
- Silent beats need an intention, not empty waiting.

### 4. Beats and Turning Points

For each consequential beat, capture: Trigger | New interpretation | Tactic | Observable behavior | Next state.

Use natural durations; a long reaction may need an uninterrupted shot, while a complex story turn may need separate generation units for dependable motion and continuity.

### 5. Interaction and Emotional Continuity

A continuity card can track:

- Pre-shot emotion intensity and quality, such as defensive anger masking fear.
- Who has power and what each thinks the other knows.
- Eye-line, body orientation, and props.
- Speaking and listening states.
- End-state and unfinished action.
- Residue carried into the next shot.

### 6. Cinematic Presentation

- Wide shot: relationship geography, isolation, distance, or environmental consequence.
- Over-shoulder: power and perspective.
- Close-up: shifts in thought, not merely dramatic tears.
- Reaction shot: the listener's changed meaning of the scene.
- Silence: a choice; use when withholding words matters.
- Sound: breathing, fabric, objects, room tone, or cutting to silence may provide emotional clarity, but should never be overprescribed in a soundless reference.

### 7. Quality Controls and Failure Conditions

Red flags:

- Crying immediately before an event has registered.
- Every line performed at maximum intensity.
- Characters not really affecting each other.
- Emotional state resets after camera cuts.
- Arbitrary gestures or repeated hand and head movements.
- Forced tears when restraint is narratively stronger.
- Actor gestures contradict spatial geography or continuity.
- Prompt is too long or contains too many movements or contradictory directions.

## AEVUM shot card

Use this internally or when requested.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Scene function | What must change in the story? |
| Character objective | What are they trying to cause? |
| Obstacle | Why can't they easily succeed? |
| Entry state | What residual emotion arrives from the previous shot? |
| Trigger | Specific perceptual or interpersonal event |
| Chosen tactic | A playable action: conceal, challenge, soothe, plead |
| Primary visible cue | Exactly one necessary action |
| Secondary cue | At most two, if really needed |
| Camera or duration | What coverage grants this reaction visibility? |
| Exit state | What has changed and must carry to the next shot? |
| Continuity locks | Eye line, orientation, wardrobe, prop, ambient details |

## Director's hierarchy of fixes

1. Fix causal structure and character motive.
2. Fix timing of stimulus and response.
3. Fix tactics, interactions, and emotional continuity.
4. Fix behavioral choices and subtlety.
5. Fix framing, rhythm, and sound support.
6. Only then refine AI prompting or regenerate isolated shots.

## Evidence standard in reviews

- Observed: “At 00:17 the actor turns away before the other line finishes.”
- Interpretation: “This weakens the sense of listening.”
- Recommendation: “Let the gaze remain for a short hesitation; turn after the partner's last word.”

Without accessible footage, label any assessment hypothetical, not an observation.
